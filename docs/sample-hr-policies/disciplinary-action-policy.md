# Disciplinary Action Policy

> **MANAGER-ONLY DOCUMENT** — This policy contains guidance intended for people managers and HR professionals. It should not be shared with employees who are subject to disciplinary proceedings.

## Purpose

This policy establishes a consistent, fair, and legally defensible framework for addressing employee performance issues and policy violations at EKAP. Consistent application of this policy is critical to protecting the company from legal liability and ensuring equitable treatment of all employees. Managers must involve HR at every stage of the disciplinary process.

## Progressive Discipline Framework

EKAP uses a progressive discipline approach designed to give employees the opportunity to correct performance or behavioral issues before more serious consequences are applied. The standard progression is as follows:

### Step 1: Verbal Warning

A verbal warning is the first formal step in addressing a performance or conduct issue. The manager must:
- Meet privately with the employee to discuss the specific issue
- Clearly explain the expected behavior or performance standard
- Describe the consequences of continued non-compliance
- Document the conversation in writing (date, issue discussed, employee response, action plan) and retain the documentation in the manager's files
- Notify HR that a verbal warning was issued

A verbal warning remains active for 6 months. If no further issues arise, it expires automatically.

### Step 2: Written Warning

If the issue persists after a verbal warning, or if the initial offense is sufficiently serious, a written warning is issued. The manager must:
- Draft a formal written warning in collaboration with HR
- Include: specific description of the issue, reference to prior discussions, clear performance expectations, timeline for improvement, and consequences of further violations
- Meet with the employee to review the written warning and obtain their signature (refusal to sign should be documented)
- Provide the employee a copy and submit the original to HR for the personnel file

A written warning remains active for 12 months.

### Step 3: Final Written Warning or Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

For continued or serious violations, a final written warning or PIP is issued. A PIP includes:
- Specific, measurable goals with defined deadlines (typically 30-90 days)
- Weekly or biweekly check-in schedule with the manager
- Required HR involvement in all PIP check-ins
- Clear statement that failure to meet PIP goals may result in termination

HR must approve all PIPs before they are issued.

### Step 4: Termination

If issues continue after a PIP or final written warning, or if the violation warrants it, employment may be terminated. All terminations must be reviewed and approved by HR and, depending on level, Legal before being communicated to the employee. Managers must never communicate a termination decision without prior HR authorization.

## Immediate Termination Offenses

Certain violations are so serious that they warrant immediate termination without progressive discipline. These include but are not limited to:

- Theft, fraud, or misappropriation of company property or funds
- Physical violence or threats of violence against any person
- Sexual harassment or assault
- Discrimination or retaliation in violation of company policy or law
- Serious breach of data security or confidentiality
- Falsification of company records or expense reports
- Reporting to work under the influence of drugs or alcohol
- Gross insubordination

Even in cases of immediate termination, HR must be consulted before any action is taken.

## Documentation Requirements

Thorough documentation is essential at every stage. Managers must document:
- Date and time of the incident or observation
- Specific behavior or performance gap (factual, not interpretive)
- Names of any witnesses
- Employee's response and explanation
- Actions agreed upon or required
- Manager's signature and date

All documentation must be submitted to HR within 2 business days of any disciplinary meeting.

## Appeals Process

Employees subject to disciplinary action have the right to appeal. The employee must submit a written appeal to HR within **5 business days** of receiving the disciplinary action. HR will review the appeal, consult with the relevant manager and department head, and provide a written response within 10 business days. The decision of HR on appeal is final.

## Manager Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for applying this policy consistently and without bias. Inconsistent application of discipline can expose EKAP to discrimination claims. Managers must consult HR before issuing any formal disciplinary action. Any manager found to have applied discipline inconsistently or retaliatorily will themselves be subject to disciplinary action.

## Record Retention

All disciplinary records are retained in the employee's personnel file for a period of **7 years** following the date of the disciplinary action or, in the case of termination, 7 years from the date of termination. Records are maintained in accordance with applicable recordkeeping laws.
